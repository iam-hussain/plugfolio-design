Group A — Auth

One minimal system for all roles. Register with email + password, verify once, then every login is a single step with no email round-trip. No handle ever appears in auth. Auth never appears on a shopping path — so these screens carry no shopper chrome and no bottom tab bar.

A0 · Auth shell

shared layout/join /signin /verify /forgot /reset

The frame all five auth routes fill. A single centred column, one job per screen, nothing to click except the one thing the screen is for. Deliberately dead-ended: no nav, no explore link, no way to wander into a shopping surface mid-auth.

Layout decisions to discuss

  • Two panes on desktop, one column on phone. The left panel is not decoration — it is where the three audiences get told apart. Creator, shopper and business each get their own headline, description and three bullets; the form beside it is byte-identical for all three. On a phone the panel collapses to a single blurb line under the tabs, because there is no room to sell and fill in a form at once.
  • The tabs belong to /join, not to the shell. On register they are a real declaration of intent that changes where verification lands you. On every other auth route they would be a control that does nothing — see A2.
  • The brand mark is the only exit. Auth must never appear on a shopping path — so the shell offers no Explore link and no tab bar. Getting out means going home.
  • Password fields always carry a show/hide toggle. The password rule is stated up front, above the field, not revealed as an error after the fact.
  • Every submit button has a busy state — disabled, label changes. In-app browsers double-fire taps; this is a correctness requirement, not polish.

A1 · /join

registerthe one screen where role is a real choice

Email + password, one verification email follows. Arrives from the landing page's three doors or as an upgrade from the claim sheet. The role tabs are load-bearing here — they set the panel, the heading, the button label, and above all where the verification link lands you. They do not create three kinds of account: every account created here is the same account.

Behaviour

  • Submit replaces the form with check-your-email in place — it is a state of this screen, not a new route, so Resend and Change email keep the entered address in hand.
  • What the role tab actually buys you — it is the only thing on the screen that changes behaviour, and it changes it after verification:
    • Creator → Connect Socials → create a profile. Connecting is what makes a handle un-squattable, so a creator cannot skip it.
    • Business → business basics → /collabs.
    • Shopper → back to the follow or comment they were attempting. Nowhere new.
  • Picking a tab does not create a kind of account. Same table, same credentials, same capabilities. A creator who later wants to hire someone gets a business by making one — not by registering again. This is the single most important thing the wireframe must not imply otherwise.
  • No handle, no username, no display name here. Email is the only login identifier the product has, and the public handle is never a login.

A2 · /signin

log inNO role tabshonors ?callbackUrl

One step, no email round-trip, ever. Two of its states are security requirements rather than tone choices, and the wireframe has to hold that line. The role tabs are deliberately absent here — see the note below; the role panel stays and is driven by ?as=.

Why no role tabs here — the one open argument

  • On /join the tab is a question we can't answer — the account doesn't exist, so only the user knows what they came to do. On /signin it is a question we can already answer better: the moment the password checks out we know every hat that email holds. Asking is worse than looking.
  • A tab that changes nothing is bad; a tab that changes something is worse. If picking "Business" routed to /collabs and the account has no business, the user gets bounced. If it routes nowhere, the control is a lie. There is no third option.
  • One of the current sublines is factually false. "Sign in to your business account" asserts a separate business account exists. It doesn't — one email, many hats. Copy that contradicts the account model is a correctness bug, not a tone preference.
  • Keep the panel, drop the tabs. The role panel still renders, driven by ?as= carried from whichever door they arrived through (default: creator). "Create an account" still forwards that role to /join?as=. Nothing visible promises anything untrue, and the screen still feels like the door it was entered from.
  • Where multi-hat accounts get sorted out is /account, which the sitemap already defines as "links to any roles this email holds". That is the role switchboard — see C1. Sign-in doesn't need to ask, because the next screen already answers.

Rules this layout enforces

  • The generic-failure message is a layout constraint, not just copy. It sits above the form as one banner. If it were rendered per-field, the position of the error would reveal whether the email exists.
  • Unverified is a different shape — a banner that carries its own action (resend). Users stuck here need a way out that isn't retyping their password.
  • ?callbackUrl returns the user where they were. With no callback, sign-in lands on /account for a multi-hat account and straight into the single surface when the account has only one.

A3 · /verify

consumes the token on load

Not really a page — a landing pad with three outcomes. It has no form and nothing to fill in, so the whole screen is one status block and at most one action.

A4 · /forgot

request a reset link

One field, one outcome. The outcome is always "check your email", whether or not the account exists — the screen must never become a way to test which emails are registered.

A5 · /reset

new passworddoubles as manager set-password

Reached from an emailed link. One form, two arrival contexts — a user resetting a forgotten password, and an invited Manager who has never had one. Only the heading and the success destination differ; the form is identical.

Note

  • Success signs the user in — neither context bounces back to /signin. A manager landing here is verified and signed straight into the managed profile.

A6 · Inline claim sheet

component, not a routeopens over the current page

The one place an anonymous shopper is ever asked for an account — and it is never a wall. It opens over the creator page when they tap Follow or the comment band, and dismissing it leaves them exactly where they were, still able to shop.

The rule this component exists to protect

  • Shopping never requires an account. This sheet may only ever appear behind Follow, Comment or Reply. It must never appear on Buy, on a coupon copy, on a product page load, or on any path toward the retailer.
  • Dismiss is always available and always cheap — the attempted action simply doesn't persist. No "are you sure", no second ask.
  • It opens over the page rather than routing, so a shopper who declines is still on the post they came for, one tap from the retailer.

Group B — Explore

The first screen wrapped in the global shopper chrome, so the chrome is wireframed here as its own screen. Explore is the discovery door for shoppers who didn't arrive via a bio link. No login anywhere on it.

B0 · Global shopper chrome

wraps every public + shopper-account screen

Top bar everywhere, four-tab bottom bar on mobile. It is a way to get home, discover and reach an account — and nothing in it may ever interrupt a buy.

Behaviour

  • Active tab is set programmatically via aria-current. Reserved top-level routes must not wrongly light SHOP.
  • HOME is active only on exact /. ACCOUNT is also active on /signin.
  • The landing page uses its own nav and footer — not this chrome.
  • Nothing here ever walls the buy path. The account slot is an entrance, never a gate.

B1 · /explore

discovery?tab= ?q=server-rendered

Search plus browse, across creators and products. Server-rendered and works without JS — the search box is a plain form. One page of results, no pagination yet.

Layout decisions to discuss

  • Order is fixed: heading → viewer note → tab switch → search → [LATER] filter row → result count → grid. The viewer note sits above the search box because it's a promise about the whole screen, not a hint about the field.
  • The tab switch is above the search box, not beside it — searching creators and searching products are different searches, and the scope has to be visible before you type.
  • Two card types, one grid. Creator cards use 4:3 media, product cards square. Same columns, same gaps, so a tab switch doesn't reflow the page shape.
  • Product cards carry up to two markers at top-left — an active coupon chip showing the code, and an own-store marker. Only one appears per card in practice; if a product is both, the coupon wins the corner and the own-store marker moves to the meta line. Worth a decision.
  • No loading state exists — the page is server-rendered. Skeletons here would be a lie.

B2 · /explore — empty states

two distinct empties

The two empties mean different things and must not share copy. One is "your search was too narrow"; the other is "the platform is new".

Behaviour

  • The query is trimmed and length-capped; matching is case-insensitive on creator username and product title.
  • The result-count line stays visible at zero ("0 creators") — it confirms the search ran.

Group C — Which role is in play

Role is a mode, not an identity. One email is shopper, creator and business at once — roles are capabilities layered on one account, never separate logins. So "which user is this?" has no answer; the answerable question is "which hat is on right now, and does the frame say so?" That gets handled at three separate moments, three different ways.

C0 · The three chromes

the primary role signal

Once someone is inside, the chrome tells them which mode they're in — not a badge on their avatar, not a colour on their name. Three surfaces already exist in the spec; what's missing is that they share one slot in the same position that names the mode and switches it.

Why this and not the obvious alternatives

  • Not a colour per role. Tempting — creator violet, business slate, shopper neutral — but it burns the one colour system the brand has on a distinction most users never make, and it breaks on /[handle], which all three roles look at simultaneously. Structure carries the mode; colour stays the brand's.
  • Not a badge on the avatar. A badge describes the person. The person is all three. What varies is the surface, so the surface should say it.
  • The three chromes already differ structurally — bottom tab bar / section tabs / neither. That difference is doing most of the work already; the mode slot just names it out loud and makes it switchable, so a creator who lands in the wrong world has one tap out.
  • Managers are not a fourth mode. A Manager lives in the creator shell on someone else's profile. The switcher lists it under Creator with the owner's handle; what changes is which controls are disabled inside Settings, not the chrome.

C1 · /account

the role switchboardshopper chrome

Already in the sitemap as "member handle + links to any roles this email holds + sign out" — which makes it the one screen whose entire job is answering which hats do I have. It is where sign-in lands when there's no callback, and it is the full-page version of the mode slot.

Rules

  • A hat this account doesn't hold does not appear as a locked card. One exception, deliberate: the "become a creator" block on a shopper-only account, because that is the product's actual growth path and this is the one screen where selling it isn't a wall. No equivalent business upsell — a business is created when someone needs one.
  • The member handle shown here is the comment identity. It is not any creator username and it never appears in auth.
  • This screen is inside the shopper chrome, ACCOUNT tab active — which is correct: you are not in creator or business mode while choosing which mode to enter.

C2 · One page, four viewers

/[handle] and product pagesalready a named rule

The hardest case: a page all three roles look at at the same time. The rule already in DESIGN.md — Viewer-Swaps-The-Chrome — is that only the header action slot and any band change. The buy path is byte-identical for every viewer. Repeated here because it is the answer to "who is in action" on the surfaces that matter most.

The test

  • If a viewer state changes what someone can buy or how they buy it, the state is wrong — not the layout. Four viewers, four different frames, one identical shop.
  • The owner does not get an editable page. Owner tools are a band and an action slot; editing happens in the dashboard. A page that becomes an editor when its owner looks at it is a page the owner can no longer trust as a preview.
  • Admin vs Manager is a control-level difference, not a viewer — same band, same slot, but Edit profile is absent for a Manager. Where a Manager is blocked inside Settings the control is shown disabled with a reason, never silently hidden.

Group D — Alternates

B1 and A0 are the safe reading of the spec: a marketplace card grid and a SaaS split-screen. Both are correct and both are anonymous — you could swap the logo for any competitor's and nothing would look wrong. These two are the same screens designed against the north star instead of around it: the product tag pinned on a photograph is the mechanism made visible, so it should be the thing discovery and signup are built from.

D1 · /explore — the tagged wall

alternate to B1bends the spec — read the notes

Kill the Creators/Products tab switch. You don't browse objects, you browse content, and the things inside it are already priced. A wall of real posts with tags visible at rest — the shop window is the feed. A creator fan sits above it for people who came looking for a person.

What this buys, and what it costs

  • The tab switch disappears. Creators and products were never two things to browse — a product only exists because it's in a post, and a creator is who made the post. Browsing the posts reaches both. That deletes a control, a URL parameter and a whole second grid.
  • Shelves replace search-as-primary. Nobody arrives at a discovery page knowing a creator's username — that's the one thing search is good at. They arrive knowing they want skincare. Shelves already exist as creator-defined categories; this is the first surface that reads them across creators.
  • Price is visible before the tap. On the B1 card grid you learn the price after committing to a card. Here every tag carries its price in the wall, so the shopper filters by budget with their eyes.
  • Cost — tag density. A post with eight products cannot show eight pills at 160px wide. Cap it at 3 and collapse the rest into a single 5 things › pill that opens the post. Needs a decision on which 3 — creator order, or cheapest-first?
  • Cost — it needs real imagery to work at all. The B1 grid degrades gracefully to placeholder rectangles; this one doesn't. On a young platform with thin content the wall looks emptier than a card grid does. That's an argument for launching with B1 and moving to D1 at content volume — or for a hybrid where the wall falls back to cards under N posts.
  • Bends the spec: §5.3 specifies a Creators/Products tab switch and two card types. This replaces both. The underlying data supports it (posts already carry tagged products with coordinates) but it is a product IA change, not just a layout one.

D2 · Auth — proof instead of pitch

alternate to A0 / A1

The violet panel with a two-line headline and three ticks is the single most reproduced layout in software. Swap the promise for the artefact: the panel is a real tagged post — the thing a creator is about to make, or the thing a shopper is about to follow. Same slot, same split, but it shows the product instead of describing it.

The second idea in here: the tabs shouldn't exist on /join either

  • The door already answered the question. Nobody reaches /join by accident — they clicked the creator door on the landing page, the business door, or upgraded from a claim sheet. ?as= is already set by the click that got them here. Showing three tabs asks a question the user just answered, and offers them the chance to answer it wrong.
  • The three roles aren't peers, and equal-weight tabs claim they are. A shopper doesn't need an account at all — that's rule one of the product. Putting Shopper next to Creator as an equal signup path actively contradicts the thing the page is trying to promise. The shopper path into /join only really exists as a claim-sheet upgrade, where the role is already known.
  • So: keep the tabs only as a fallback. Land on /join cold with no ?as= and you get a chooser. Arrive through a door — which is every real journey — and you get that door's artefact and no tabs. One control, shown to the ~nobody who needs it.
  • Cost: the panel now needs real content per role, and PRODUCT.md forbids fabricating creators, handles, products or prices. So it must ship as either visibly-placeholder imagery or genuine opted-in creator content. That's a real constraint on this direction and the reason A0 is the safer build.

Group E — Creator page layouts & ad slots

Two independent choices already ship in creator-prefs.js: a header treatment (Compact · Balanced · Centred) and a post layout (Grid · Cards · List). They compose, so the creator has 9 combinations, not 3. E1 and E2 wireframe each axis; E3 proposes two more layouts; E4 covers the ad slots, which are new product scope.

E1 · Header treatments

creator picksalready in creator-prefs.js

Only the identity block changes. Shelves, grid and comments below are untouched by this choice — which is what keeps 9 combinations maintainable instead of 9 separate pages.

Rules

  • The header choice never touches the buy path. Shelves, grid, tags and prices are identical across all three, so a creator cannot accidentally make their page harder to shop.
  • Centred is the one that costs vertical space on a phone. It pushes the first post row below the fold — worth stating in Settings so the choice is informed rather than aesthetic.
  • The accent is the creator's only colour. The role gradient in the hero band is Plugfolio's and does not change per creator.

E2 · Post layouts

Grid · Cards · Listalready shipping

The second axis. Same posts, same tags, three densities — each suits a different kind of content, and the trade is how many posts fit against how much each one says.

E3 · Two layouts worth adding

proposalnot built

Grid, Cards and List are all one flat sequence of posts. These two change the structure rather than the density, which is where a creator's page can actually start to differ.

What each one needs that doesn't exist yet

  • Feature needs a "pin this post" control in the tagging editor, and a rule for what shows when nothing is pinned (fall back to newest).
  • Shelves only works for a creator with 2+ categories and enough posts in each. Below that it looks broken, so it should be offered disabled with a reason until they qualify — never silently missing.
  • Both still respect the rule that the choice cannot change what is buyable. They reorder posts; they never hide any.

E4 · Ad slots

NEW SCOPEfunds the Pro upgrade

Plugfolio-sold ads on free creator pages, removed by Pro. This collides with the product's first principle — the shopper's path is sacred — and with the creator's credibility, which is the thing being sold. The placements below are chosen so an ad never sits between a tap and the retailer, and never sits where a creator's own recommendation would.

The rules an ad slot has to obey here

  • Never on the outbound path. No interstitial, no ad between tapping Buy and the retailer, no ad above the Buy button. If attribution recording must never block a redirect, an ad certainly must not.
  • Never shaped like a tagged product. No product-tag pill, no price in tabular figures, no "Buy" label, no violet or lime dot. An ad that borrows the tag vocabulary is claiming the creator recommended it.
  • Always labelled AD · PLUGFOLIO in Micro, plus a "why am I seeing this" affordance. The product already labels what it can and cannot measure; this is the same promise applied to who is paying.
  • One slot per page. Not one per scroll depth. The cap is the product decision — the rest is inventory pressure, and inventory pressure is how shopper surfaces rot.
  • Free pages only, and the owner sees it. The slot doubles as the Pro upsell, in place, on the real page. A creator should never discover their own page carries ads by hearing it from a follower.
  • Open question — does a Pro creator's page still carry Plugfolio's ad? If Pro is bought by the creator, the answer is no, and the revenue model has to survive that. Worth settling before any of this is designed further.

Product spec conflicts to resolve

  • PRODUCT.md lists no advertising and no paid tier. Both are new, and Pro implies on-platform payment, which v1 explicitly excludes. The subscription may sit outside the "no money in v1" rule, but that has to be a stated decision, not an assumption.
  • An ad on a creator's page spends the creator's audience. Whether creators can opt out without paying — and what that does to the model — is a policy question, not a layout one.

Group F — How it works · For creators · For business

Three dedicated routes, each owning one audience and one search surface. Two of the three links are currently broken as destinations: For creators points at /signin, which is a form for people who already have an account, and For business points at /collabs, which drops a stranger into a logged-out product surface. These pages replace both.

The cost to plan for: §5.2 puts this content on the landing page. With real pages the landing sections become short teasers that link out, so each argument is written once. If both carry the full pitch they will drift, and the FAQ structured data will end up contradicting itself across two URLs.

F1 · /how-it-works

shopper-facingcarries the FAQ structured data

The only one of the three with genuinely explanatory content. Its real job is answering the question a shopper arriving from a bio link actually has, which is whether this is safe.

Content, all of it already true

  • Nothing here is a claim that needs inventing. The loop, the three rules and the tracked/not-tracked split are all in PRODUCT.md, which is why this page can be written without a single fabricated number.
  • Naming the unmeasured thing is the argument. A page that only lists what it tracks reads like every other affiliate tool. Saying in-store redemption is not tracked is what makes the tracked figures credible.
  • The FAQ answers and the visible copy have to stay identical — the spec requires the structured data to restate what is on screen.

F2 · /for-creators

replaces the link to /signin

Aimed at someone who does not have an account yet, which is exactly why pointing this at the sign-in form was wrong. Ends at /join?as=creator.

Why the limit gets its own block

  • Creators have been burned by platforms that hold funds. Stating up front that money never touches Plugfolio removes the question before it is asked, and it is a real differentiator rather than a disclaimer.
  • Every figure shown carries the tracked label. A creator-facing page that shows an untagged number teaches creators to distrust the dashboard.
  • The 5-profile and 3-manager caps belong here too, stated as facts. Someone planning to run ten brands should find that out now.

F3 · /for-business

replaces the link to /collabs

A pitch, not the product. /collabs is the working surface and should be reached after signing up, not before.

Notes

  • Naming the exclusions is a filter, not a weakness. A brand expecting media kits and campaign reporting will churn on day two; telling them here costs one sign-up and saves a support thread.
  • Budgets stay free text everywhere, including any example on this page — the product deliberately does not validate a currency, and an example showing "$" would imply otherwise to an Indian brand.
  • No customer names, logos or testimonials: none exist, and PRODUCT.md treats inventing them as a correctness failure rather than a content shortcut.
